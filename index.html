Quaker worship is simple. We gather in expectant silence, without clergy or a prepared service. Anyone may speak if they feel deeply led to do so. You do not need to be a Quaker — or know anything about Quakers — to join us.

Your first visit

What happens in a Quaker meeting?

We sit together in silence, listening inwardly. The silence is not empty; it is a shared space for reflection, prayer, and openness. From time to time someone may stand and offer a short message. After about an hour, the meeting closes and conversation follows.

There is no collection plate and no expectation that visitors participate beyond being present.

“There is something of God in everyone.”

About Friends

A faith rooted in experience.

Quakers, also known as the Religious Society of Friends, seek to live with integrity, simplicity, equality, peace, community, and care for the earth. Quaker practice places emphasis on direct spiritual experience and discernment together in community.

Curious?

The best introduction is simply to visit. There is no test of belief and no pressure to join. Sit with us, experience the silence, and stay for conversation afterward if you wish.
